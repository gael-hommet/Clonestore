## What is NOT concluded here
- Whether Pierre "is" a high-risk AI system under Art. 6 — this requires comparing the *exact* deployed configuration (which sub-capabilities are actually live vs. `MISSING` in the P8.10 canon) against the Annex III conditions and any applicable exemptions (e.g. purely preparatory/narrow procedural task carve-outs), which only a qualified practitioner should certify.
- Whether the many coded human-only floors are, on their own, sufficient to change the classification — they are strong risk-mitigation evidence, not a substitute for the legal determination itself.
- Whether CloneStore (as the AI provider) or its business customers (as deployers) bear which specific Art. 6/Art. 9-onward obligations (risk management, technical documentation, logging, human oversight measures, conformity assessment) — this allocation depends on the actual commercial relationship and must be confirmed by counsel before the 2026-08-02 compliance date.

## What IS established, code-verified fact
Four independent, redundant governance layers (CloneGuard ×2, `hr/autonomy.ts`, P16A sensitive-floor, the B47 legal taxonomy) all converge on the same set of hard human-only actions (dismissal, disciplinary decision/sanction, final recruitment decision, promotion, salary change, medical/legal conclusions, protected-characteristic assessments) — this is real, structural, and already stronger than most comparable HR-AI products, but is a *mitigating control*, not itself an AI Act compliance certificate.
